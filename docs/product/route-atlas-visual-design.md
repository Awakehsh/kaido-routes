# Route track map visual design

Status: accepted design contract, 2026-08-03. This replaces the earlier
semantic-zoom rectilinear atlas design. The direction was validated with an
owner-reviewed design study rendering the C2 inner-loop circuit from the real
whole-network snapshot; that study is a presentation snapshot and grants no
route, navigation, or passage authority. The earlier design's licensing,
recognition-reference, release-gate, and journey-overlay facts remain current
and are carried below.

## Product role

The track map is the Kaido-owned whole-route presentation of one selected
route. One persistent control switches between it and the provider-backed
geographic driving map while planning or driving; the selection persists and
is never forced by journey phase. The track map is generated by one
route-agnostic renderer from the selected route and its exact bound network
snapshot. It is never hand-authored per route: every route a user can select —
any of the 26 official routes, loop circuits composed across routes,
radial-to-loop joins, and repeated laps — must render through the same pipeline.

## Rendering contract

1. **Whole route, one frame.** The entire selected route stays visible in one
   readable frame, parked and driving. No zoom is required to understand the
   route; zoom, if offered, is a convenience and never hides information.
2. **Stylized continuous line.** The route renders as one continuous stylized
   line derived from geometry bound to the selected RoutePlan (simplification
   and smoothing allowed).
   Orientation and proportion may be adjusted for legibility and need not be
   north-up or geographically proportional. Directed connectivity, route
   occurrence order, facility direction, and component-route identity are
   never distorted.
3. **Every on-route facility, always labeled.** All IC, JCT, and PA on the
   selected route carry visible labels at all times — labels never appear or
   disappear with zoom. A leader returns each spaced label to its exact route
   point. JCT labels are emphasized; the next reviewed decision is highlighted.
   Facility names follow the interface locale while Japanese sign text and
   route shields remain available. Off-route network appears only as quiet
   context or not at all.
4. **Component routes stay honest.** A circuit that requires more than one
   official route exposes each component visually (for example a practical C2
   circuit shows its C2 and Bayshore legs distinctly with their route
   shields); the renderer never draws a falsely self-contained ring.
5. **Explicit entrance and exit.** The directional entrance and planned exit
   are explicit marks with neutral copy. Their direction validity (for example
   inner-loop-only, ETC-only) comes from operator facility facts, never from
   geometry inference.
6. **Laps are occurrences.** Repeated traversals render as separate ordered
   occurrences with visible ordinal and count — offset, never deduplicated.
   Whole-journey progress accounts for the full occurrence sequence.
7. **Progress states are actor-owned.** Passed, current, future, and skipped
   occurrence states come only from the exact actor-owned navigation snapshot.
   The position marker is route-bound progress with explicit confidence;
   tunnel or low-confidence positioning is shown as estimated and never
   fabricates precision. Before activation every occurrence is planned-state.
   While driving, the current position is the most prominent point element on
   the track map: it renders above every other layer, is never occluded by
   labels, leaders, or facility marks (nearby labels yield), stays clearly
   visible at arm's length against both route colors, and carries a travel
   direction indicator. Its estimated/degraded state changes its appearance
   explicitly rather than shrinking or hiding it.
8. **Direction is visible.** Travel-direction chevrons mark the remaining
   path.
9. **Uncertainty is visible.** Toll and passage information renders its
   explicit unconfirmed state when no current source exists.
10. **No performance framing.** No speed, lap-time, ranking, or racing
    elements anywhere in the presentation.

## Shape families

The renderer handles two families through the same pipeline:

- **Loop circuits** (C1, C2 plus Bayshore, other closed compositions): labels
  occupy four quadrant zones around the ring; direction chevrons follow the
  loop tangent.
- **Linear routes** (radial and Kanagawa lines, or any open path): labels
  occupy the two corridor sides; the entrance anchors one end and the exit
  the other.

Radial-to-loop journeys combine both: the radial approach renders as a spur
joining the loop at its legal JCT movement, in route order.

## Data authority and release gate

The track map presents exact bound data; it cannot create route or navigation
authority.

- Candidate planning and labeled replay bind to the bundled network snapshot,
  the complete RoutePlan, and its neutral runtime-asset identity. This does not
  create a Route Atlas or joint product release. Release-backed navigation must
  instead bind to one accepted Route Atlas release and the exact active network
  snapshot, directed topology, legal successors, and occurrence bindings. A
  coordinate crossing never establishes a connection.
- Selecting a segment or facility explains it or submits a stable reviewed
  choice to the route editor; a gesture never snaps into an unreviewed
  movement, and neither projection can mutate an entrance, JCT movement, PA
  access, exit, or RoutePlan occurrence.
- For release-backed navigation, `RouteAtlasJourneyProjector` remains the
  implemented renderer-neutral boundary between a release and the phone
  presentation. It projects planned occurrences before activation and accepts
  only the exact actor-owned snapshot partition afterwards; unknown,
  duplicated, reordered, or mismatched progress blocks the projection.
  Multiple occurrences may reference the same segment; the renderer offsets
  them and exposes ordinal and count.
- Source and licence attribution remains adjacent to the rendered map.

## Visual language

- one muted line family for inactive context;
- compact Kaido-owned route-code capsules, distinct from operator artwork;
- warm route color for the active plan only, with component legs
  distinguishable;
- cyan for an eligible measured position;
- coral for recovery or blocking states;
- neutral, action-first copy; no speed, lap-time, ranking, or racing language.

## Recognition reference

The pre-release design study may combine two non-navigable references: MLIT
N06-2025 current-state geometry for the full-network silhouette, and the
current operator route-mark table for factual Japanese route names and codes.
A route mark may appear only when its operator name has an explicit match to
one MLIT context route and its anchor snaps to a vertex of that matched route.
The deterministic output
`data/route-atlas/design/shuto-route-atlas-recognition-reference.svg` (86
retained source paths, 26 matched route identities, 28 snapped route-code
marks) carries visible MLIT / CC BY 4.0 attribution and explicit `REVIEW_ONLY`
and `navigation_authority=false` metadata. It is a recognition reference, not
selectable topology, direction, legal movement, or route authority, and it
cannot unlock route selection, highlighting, positioning, recovery, or
guidance.

## Retained K7 fixture

The K7 Aoba-to-Kohoku exit-handoff release, its schematic layout candidate,
and their dated evidence records remain retained deterministic fixtures under
`docs/testing/k7-*.md`, `docs/updates/`, and `data/route-atlas/`. They are not
a delivery track. Any evidence view that displays the K7 candidate must keep
its `LEGAL REVIEW INCOMPLETE` state and surface-terminal boundary disclosures
exactly as those records state them; the unresolved surface-way identity is
not treated as satisfied and cannot be reused as evidence elsewhere.
